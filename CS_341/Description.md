# CS341 - Mechanized Mathematical Inference
**University of Illinois (Historical Reconstruction - circa 1994)** "The Engineering of Reasoning,"

> "Can reasoning itself become computation?"


# The Central Question

Throughout this course students should continually ask one question.

> Can reasoning itself be represented as computation?

Every topic introduced during the semester represents a different answer to
that question.

Modern AI systems continue to explore this question using statistical methods,
large language models, symbolic reasoning, and hybrid architectures.

Although implementations have changed dramatically, the underlying problem
remains one of the central questions of Computer Science.
---

# Course Description

CS341 introduces students to one of the central questions of Artificial
Intelligence:

Can logical reasoning be represented as a computational process?

The course examines the mathematical foundations of automated reasoning,
predicate logic, theorem proving, unification, and symbolic inference.
Students learn how computers manipulate logical expressions to derive new
knowledge and prove mathematical statements.

Unlike programming courses that emphasize implementation, CS341 focuses on
the structure of reasoning itself.

Students develop an understanding of formal logic not as philosophy, but as
an engineering discipline used to construct intelligent systems.

Programming assignments emphasize symbolic manipulation and automated theorem
proving using LISP and PROLOG.

---

# Course Objectives

Upon completion students should be able to

- Explain the relationship between logic and computation.
- Translate real-world problems into formal logical expressions.
- Apply predicate logic to represent knowledge.
- Understand unification algorithms.
- Describe automated theorem proving.
- Explain the Resolution Principle.
- Compare forward and backward reasoning.
- Evaluate the strengths and limitations of symbolic inference.
- Understand the historical relationship between logic programming and AI.

# Learning Philosophy

CS341 teaches students that computation extends beyond arithmetic.

Reasoning itself can be represented as symbolic manipulation governed by
formal mathematical rules.

The goal is not merely to prove theorems but to understand how intelligent
systems derive conclusions from incomplete knowledge.

# Modern Perspective

Although contemporary Artificial Intelligence frequently emphasizes machine
learning and neural networks, symbolic reasoning continues to play an
important role in enterprise systems.

Modern applications include

- policy engines
- business rules
- automated verification
- constraint solvers
- symbolic planners
- AI tool orchestration
- hybrid reasoning systems

Students completing CS341 should recognize that formal logic remains one of
the foundational languages through which intelligent systems represent and
reason about knowledge.
