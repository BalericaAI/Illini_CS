# CS348 - Introduction to Artificial Intelligence
**University of Illinois (Historical Reconstruction - circa 1994)**

> "Before a machine can learn, it must first reason.
> Before it can reason, it must first represent knowledge."

---

# Course Description

CS348 serves as the intellectual foundation for the Artificial Intelligence
curriculum.

Unlike modern introductory AI courses that begin with neural networks and
large language models, this course introduces Artificial Intelligence as the
science of representing knowledge, reasoning under constraints, searching
complex problem spaces, and constructing intelligent software systems.

Students learn the major branches of AI as they existed during the late
1980s and early 1990s while simultaneously understanding how those ideas
continue to influence modern enterprise AI systems.

The course emphasizes ideas rather than tools.

Programming assignments are primarily completed in LISP to reinforce symbolic
computation and recursive thinking.

---

# Course Objectives

Upon completion of this course students should be able to:

- Explain the historical development of Artificial Intelligence.
- Describe symbolic AI and statistical AI.
- Implement search algorithms.
- Build simple inference systems.
- Represent knowledge using symbolic structures.
- Understand heuristic reasoning.
- Compare expert systems with machine learning.
- Explain why LISP became the language of AI research.
- Understand the intellectual progression toward modern AI systems.

# Learning Philosophy

This course intentionally emphasizes understanding over implementation.

Students should understand why a technique was invented before studying its
modern implementation.

The objective is to think like an AI researcher from 1994 while recognizing
the continuity of ideas leading to present-day intelligent systems.

# Modern Perspective

Although this course reflects AI education during the early 1990s, the
fundamental questions remain relevant today.

Modern systems continue to rely upon

- search
- reasoning
- representation
- planning
- uncertainty
- learning

Large Language Models and Agentic AI extend these ideas rather than replacing
them.

Students completing this course should recognize that modern AI is best
understood as an evolution of sixty years of research rather than an entirely
new discipline.
