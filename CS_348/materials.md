# Recommended Books

The books in this course are divided into three categories.

1. Core Texts
2. Foundational References
3. Historical Classics

Students are not expected to read every book cover-to-cover.

Instead, each book provides a different perspective on Artificial Intelligence.
Together they illustrate the evolution of AI from symbolic reasoning to modern
intelligent systems.

---

# Core Texts

These books form the primary intellectual foundation of the course.

---

## Patrick Henry Winston

### Artificial Intelligence (3rd Edition)

Role

Primary course text.

Importance

Winston provides one of the clearest introductions to classical Artificial
Intelligence ever written. Rather than emphasizing programming syntax, Winston
teaches students how AI researchers think about search, reasoning, planning,
knowledge representation, and learning.

Students should pay particular attention to the historical motivations behind
each algorithm.

Modern Connection

Although published before deep learning, many concepts introduced by Winston
remain fundamental to modern AI systems, including search, symbolic reasoning,
planning, and knowledge representation.

---

## Nils J. Nilsson

### Principles of Artificial Intelligence

Role

Theoretical foundation.

Importance

Nilsson presents AI from a mathematical and algorithmic perspective. His work
emphasizes heuristic search, reasoning, and formal problem solving.

Students should compare Nilsson's treatment of search algorithms with modern
AI planning systems.

Modern Connection

Planning agents and autonomous systems continue to employ many of the search
strategies introduced in this text.

# Foundational References

These books expand specific areas introduced throughout the semester.

---

## Robert Kowalski

### Logic for Problem Solving

Relationship

Preparation for CS341.

Importance

Introduces predicate logic, automated reasoning, and theorem proving.

Students interested in symbolic AI, Prolog, and logical inference should study
this text carefully.

Modern Connection

Large Language Models frequently perform reasoning tasks that can be compared
with classical symbolic inference systems.

---

## Ronald Brachman & Hector Levesque

### Readings in Knowledge Representation

Relationship

Preparation for CS342.

Importance

This collection introduced many of the foundational papers that established
knowledge representation as a scientific discipline.

Topics include

- semantic networks
- frames
- ontologies
- conceptual structures
- representation languages

Modern Connection

Knowledge graphs, enterprise ontologies, and Retrieval Augmented Generation
(RAG) all build upon these ideas.

---

## Richard Duda & Peter Hart

### Pattern Classification and Scene Analysis

Relationship

Preparation for CS346.

Importance

One of the earliest comprehensive treatments of pattern recognition.

Students should recognize how machine learning evolved from pattern
classification research.

Modern Connection

Classification remains central to modern AI despite dramatic advances in
learning algorithms.

---

## Peter Jackson

### Introduction to Expert Systems

Relationship

Preparation for CS347.

Importance

Provides a practical introduction to expert systems, production rules,
inference engines, certainty factors, and explanation facilities.

Students should compare expert systems with contemporary AI agents.

Modern Connection

Rule engines, policy systems, and AI orchestration frameworks continue many
ideas introduced in expert systems.

# Suggested Reading Order

Students unfamiliar with Artificial Intelligence should read

1. Winston
2. Nilsson
3. Jackson
4. Kowalski
5. Brachman
6. Duda & Hart
7. Pearl
8. Mitchell

The goal is not to memorize algorithms but to understand how ideas developed
over time.
