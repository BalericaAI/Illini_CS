# CS347 - Knowledge-Based Programming
**University of Illinois (Historical Reconstruction - circa 1994)**

> "Knowledge has little value until it can be organized into systems that
reason, explain, and assist human decision making."

---

# Course Description

CS347 examines the engineering principles required to design, construct,
maintain, and evolve knowledge-based software systems.

Building upon formal reasoning (CS341), knowledge acquisition (CS342), and
pattern recognition (CS346), students learn how to transform human expertise
into practical software capable of assisting real-world decision making.

Topics include expert system architecture, inference engines, rule-based
programming, explanation facilities, certainty management, knowledge base
maintenance, and enterprise deployment.

Unlike previous courses that focus upon individual techniques, CS347
emphasizes complete system design.

Students develop knowledge-based applications that integrate reasoning,
knowledge representation, and human expertise into coherent enterprise
solutions.

---

# Course Objectives

Upon successful completion students should be able to

- Design complete expert systems.
- Build production rule knowledge bases.
- Construct inference engines.
- Develop explanation facilities.
- Manage conflicting rules.
- Evaluate certainty and confidence.
- Maintain evolving knowledge bases.
- Integrate knowledge systems with traditional software.
- Explain the strengths and limitations of expert systems.

# Learning Philosophy

Knowledge-based programming is not simply writing software.

It is the disciplined engineering of systems that represent, reason about,
and explain expert knowledge.

Students should understand that explanation, maintainability, and trust are
as important as correctness.

# Modern Perspective

Many contemporary AI systems employ techniques that resemble classical
knowledge-based systems.

Policy engines, business rule systems, retrieval pipelines, enterprise
knowledge graphs, and multi-agent orchestration continue principles developed
during the expert systems era.

Students completing CS347 should recognize that modern enterprise AI often
combines symbolic reasoning, statistical learning, and human expertise within
integrated architectures.
