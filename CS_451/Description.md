# CS451 - Ontology and Integrity
**University of Illinois (Modern Graduate Course)**

> "An intelligent system must not only reason correctly.
> It must preserve the integrity of its own understanding."

---

# Course Description

CS451 investigates one of the central engineering challenges of autonomous
intelligent systems:

**How can an intelligent system evolve while preserving the integrity of its
knowledge, reasoning, and identity?**

Building upon formal reasoning (CS341), knowledge acquisition (CS342),
machine learning (CS346), knowledge-based programming (CS347), reasoning
under uncertainty (CS349), artificial neural networks (CS442), and
computational cognitive architectures (CS448), students investigate the
long-term behavior of intelligent systems operating within dynamic enterprise
environments.

The course examines ontology engineering, knowledge evolution, belief
revision, provenance, temporal reasoning, adaptive systems, cognitive
integrity, self-monitoring, and guided self-organization.

Students design architectures capable of evaluating not only external
knowledge, but also the continuing reliability of their own internal
representations.

Emphasis is placed upon engineering intelligent systems that remain coherent,
trustworthy, explainable, and operationally stable despite continuous
learning, changing environments, evolving regulations, and organizational
growth.

Throughout the semester students explore one fundamental engineering question:

> **Should an intelligent system trust what it has become after years of
learning and operation?**

---

# Course Objectives

Upon successful completion students should be able to

- Design formal ontologies for enterprise systems.
- Analyze ontology evolution over time.
- Implement belief revision strategies.
- Evaluate semantic consistency.
- Design knowledge provenance architectures.
- Measure ontology drift.
- Distinguish learning from corruption.
- Engineer self-monitoring intelligent systems.
- Evaluate adaptive cognitive architectures.
- Design long-term integrity frameworks for enterprise AI.

---

# Learning Philosophy

Knowledge is not static.

Ontologies evolve.

Organizations change.

Regulations change.

Humans change.

Intelligent systems must therefore preserve not merely information,
but the integrity of their understanding.

Students should recognize that trustworthy cognition depends upon continual
evaluation of

- identity,
- meaning,
- provenance,
- confidence,
- adaptation,
- and internal consistency.

Ontology therefore becomes an engineering discipline rather than merely a
philosophical exercise.
