# Recommended Books

CS349 examines one of the oldest and most difficult questions in Artificial
Intelligence:

> How does an intelligent system determine what knowledge deserves to be
> trusted?

Unlike previous courses, which focus upon reasoning, knowledge acquisition,
or implementation, CS349 investigates certainty itself.

Students examine deterministic reasoning, probabilistic reasoning,
knowledge provenance, confidence, evidence, and trust within distributed
knowledge environments.

The books below illustrate the historical evolution of certainty from
classical logic through modern enterprise AI.

---

# I. Historical Foundations

---

## Aristotle

### Organon

Role

The origins of deductive certainty.

Importance

Students revisit Aristotle to compare absolute logical truth with the
uncertain reasoning required by modern intelligent systems.

Central Question

When is certainty possible?

---

## Thomas Bayes

### An Essay Towards Solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances

Role

The birth of probabilistic reasoning.

Importance

Introduces belief updating based upon evidence.

Modern Connection

Every modern AI confidence score ultimately descends from Bayesian thinking.

---

## George Pólya

### Mathematics and Plausible Reasoning
(Volumes I & II)

Role

Reasoning under incomplete information.

Importance

Pólya demonstrates that many real-world decisions depend upon plausible
rather than deductive reasoning.

Students should compare plausible reasoning with expert judgment.

---

# II. Core Course Texts

---

## Judea Pearl

### Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems

Role

Primary course text.

Importance

One of the foundational works on Bayesian Networks and reasoning under
uncertainty.

Students study

- Bayesian inference
- causal reasoning
- evidence propagation
- uncertainty

Relationship

Bridge between classical Expert Systems and probabilistic AI.

---

## A. Philip Dawid
## Robert G. Cowell
## Steffen L. Lauritzen

### Probabilistic Networks and Expert Systems

Role

Companion text.

Importance

Demonstrates how probabilistic graphical models extend classical
knowledge-based systems.

Students compare

- certainty factors
- Bayesian reasoning
- probabilistic graphical models

---

## Bruce Buchanan
## Edward Shortliffe

### Rule-Based Expert Systems

Role

Historical perspective.

Importance

Examines MYCIN and the development of certainty factors.

Students compare MYCIN's certainty factors with Bayesian approaches.

---

# III. Knowledge Representation

---

## Ronald Brachman
## Hector Levesque

### Readings in Knowledge Representation

Role

Knowledge and certainty.

Importance

Trust depends upon how knowledge is represented.

Students evaluate whether poor representation can undermine confidence.

---

## John F. Sowa

### Conceptual Structures

Role

Semantic integrity.

Importance

Introduces conceptual graphs that later influence ontology engineering.

Relationship

Preparation for CS451.

---

# IV. Trust and Assurance

---

## David Bell
## Leonard LaPadula

### Secure Computer Systems:
### Unified Exposition and Multics Interpretation

Role

Historical assurance.

Importance

Although written for operating systems, Bell-LaPadula demonstrates how
trust can be formalized through policy.

Students compare security assurance with knowledge assurance.

---

## Department of Defense

### Trusted Computer System Evaluation Criteria
(The Orange Book)

Role

Engineering trust.

Importance

Students examine how operating systems earned trust through measurable
assurance requirements.

Discussion

Can similar assurance principles be developed for AI?

---

# V. Advanced Reading

---

## Stuart Russell
## Peter Norvig

### Artificial Intelligence:
### A Modern Approach

Selected Chapters

- Uncertainty
- Bayesian Networks
- Decision Theory
- Probabilistic Reasoning

Role

Modern synthesis.

Importance

Provides historical continuity from expert systems toward modern AI.

---

## Glenn Shafer

### A Mathematical Theory of Evidence

Role

Evidence theory.

Importance

Introduces Dempster-Shafer Theory for reasoning under uncertainty.

Students compare

- Bayesian inference
- Certainty Factors
- Evidence Theory

---

# Suggested Reading Order

Students should read

1. Aristotle
2. Bayes
3. Pólya
4. Buchanan & Shortliffe
5. Pearl
6. Dawid, Cowell & Lauritzen
7. Brachman & Levesque
8. Sowa
9. Orange Book
10. Shafer

The objective is to observe how engineering gradually moved from certainty,
to probability, to evidence, and ultimately toward trust.
