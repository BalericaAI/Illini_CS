# Recommended Books

Computer Models of Cognitive Processes investigates one of the oldest
questions in Artificial Intelligence:

> How can intelligent behavior emerge from the interaction of memory,
> perception, learning, reasoning, and action?

Unlike previous courses that focus upon individual AI techniques, CS448
examines complete cognitive systems.

Students compare symbolic architectures, connectionist models, hybrid
approaches, and computational theories of cognition while developing an
engineering perspective on intelligent behavior.

---

# I. Foundations of Cognition

---

## Allen Newell

### Unified Theories of Cognition

Role

Primary historical text.

Importance

Perhaps the single most important book for understanding cognitive
architectures.

Students examine

- problem solving
- memory
- learning
- production systems
- unified cognitive theory

Modern Connection

Foundation for SOAR and many later cognitive architectures.

---

## Herbert A. Simon

### The Sciences of the Artificial

Role

Engineering philosophy.

Importance

Simon argues that intelligent systems are designed artifacts rather than
natural phenomena.

Students should understand bounded rationality, hierarchy, and design as
engineering principles.

---

## John R. Anderson

### Cognitive Psychology and Its Implications

Role

Human cognition.

Importance

Introduces memory, perception, learning, and reasoning from a computational
perspective.

Relationship

Preparation for ACT-R.

---

# II. Cognitive Architectures

---

## John R. Anderson

### ACT-R

Selected Papers

Role

Computational cognition.

Importance

Students study one of the most influential cognitive architectures ever
developed.

Topics include

- declarative memory
- procedural memory
- production systems
- attention

---

## Allen Newell

### SOAR

Selected Papers

Role

Unified cognitive architecture.

Importance

Students compare SOAR with ACT-R while examining complete intelligent agents.

---

# III. Knowledge Representation

---

## Marvin Minsky

### The Society of Mind

Role

Distributed cognition.

Importance

Minsky proposes that intelligence emerges from the interaction of many
specialized processes rather than one central mechanism.

Students compare Society of Mind with modern multi-agent architectures.

Modern Connection

A fascinating precursor to modern agent orchestration.

---

## Roger Schank

### Dynamic Memory

Role

Memory organization.

Importance

Introduces scripts, episodic memory, and experience-based reasoning.

Students compare human memory with enterprise knowledge systems.

---

## Roger Schank

### Tell Me a Story

Role

Narrative cognition.

Importance

Explores how stories organize human knowledge and reasoning.

Students discuss narrative as an organizational structure for memory.

---

# IV. Connectionist Perspectives

---

## Rumelhart
## McClelland

### Parallel Distributed Processing

Volumes I & II

Role

Connectionist cognition.

Importance

Shows how distributed neural representations relate to human cognition.

Students compare symbolic cognition with distributed learning.

---

## Andy Clark

### Being There

Role

Embodied cognition.

Importance

Challenges purely symbolic views of intelligence.

Students discuss whether cognition extends beyond the brain into the
environment.

---

# V. Philosophy

---

## Daniel Dennett

### Consciousness Explained

Role

Philosophy of mind.

Importance

Students examine computational perspectives on consciousness and intentional
systems.

Discussion

Which aspects of cognition require consciousness?

---

## Daniel Dennett

### The Intentional Stance

Role

Reasoning about intelligent systems.

Importance

Introduces the idea that intelligent behavior can often be understood by
treating systems as rational agents.

Relationship

Preparation for multi-agent systems.

---

## Karl Popper

### Objective Knowledge

Role

Knowledge growth.

Importance

Students compare scientific discovery with machine learning and cognitive
adaptation.

---

# VI. Artificial Intelligence

---

## Stuart Russell
## Peter Norvig

### Artificial Intelligence:
### A Modern Approach

Selected Chapters

Role

Modern synthesis.

Importance

Provides continuity between symbolic AI, probabilistic reasoning, and modern
intelligent systems.

---

## Patrick Winston

### Artificial Intelligence

Role

Classical AI.

Importance

Students compare cognitive models with traditional AI methods.

---

# VII. Advanced Reading

---

## Douglas Hofstadter

### Gödel, Escher, Bach

Role

Patterns of thought.

Importance

An extraordinary exploration of recursion, analogy, emergence, and
intelligence.

Students should not expect easy reading.

They should expect profound questions.

---

## Douglas Hofstadter

### Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies

Role

Analogy-making.

Importance

Investigates one of the most difficult aspects of intelligence:
creative conceptual change.

Relationship

Preparation for CS451.

---

# Suggested Reading Order

1. Simon
2. Newell
3. Anderson
4. Minsky
5. Schank
6. Rumelhart & McClelland
7. Andy Clark
8. Dennett
9. Popper
10. Hofstadter
11. Russell & Norvig

Students should observe how theories of cognition evolved from symbolic
reasoning toward integrated cognitive architectures capable of perception,
learning, memory, planning, and adaptation.
