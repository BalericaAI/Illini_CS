# ECE452 - Applied Cognitive Engineering
**University of Illinois (Graduate Capstone)**

> "Engineering is not the construction of intelligent systems.
> Engineering is the construction of organizations capable of thinking."

---

# Course Description

ECE452 serves as the capstone course for the Artificial Intelligence and
Cognitive Systems curriculum.

Students integrate formal reasoning, knowledge representation, machine
learning, expert systems, probabilistic reasoning, neural computation,
cognitive architectures, ontology engineering, and enterprise systems into
the design of large-scale intelligent organizations.

Rather than studying individual AI algorithms, students investigate how
multiple intelligent systems cooperate within complex enterprises while
maintaining trust, knowledge integrity, operational resilience, and
architectural coherence over years of continuous operation.

The course examines distributed cognition, enterprise architecture,
multi-agent systems, knowledge brokers, trust brokers, semantic integration,
governance, cybernetics, organizational learning, and cognitive engineering.

Students design enterprise cognitive architectures capable of coordinating
humans, autonomous agents, knowledge systems, and evolving organizational
objectives.

Emphasis is placed upon designing organizations that remain adaptable,
trustworthy, explainable, and economically valuable despite changing
technology, changing regulations, and changing business environments.

Students complete a year-long engineering project that synthesizes concepts
from every previous course in the curriculum while producing a practical
architecture suitable for deployment within industry or government.

---

# Course Objectives

Upon successful completion students should be able to

- Design enterprise cognitive architectures.
- Engineer distributed knowledge systems.
- Coordinate multiple intelligent agents.
- Design knowledge broker architectures.
- Design trust broker architectures.
- Evaluate enterprise ontology.
- Integrate symbolic and neural AI.
- Engineer resilient cognitive infrastructures.
- Develop governance strategies for enterprise AI.
- Communicate architectural decisions to technical and executive audiences.

---

# Learning Philosophy

An Artificial Intelligence system rarely succeeds in isolation.

Successful intelligent enterprises emerge from the coordinated interaction of

- people,
- knowledge,
- autonomous agents,
- organizational processes,
- governance,
- and engineering discipline.

Students should therefore learn to design intelligent organizations rather
than merely intelligent software.

# Laboratory Philosophy

Students will not complete isolated programming assignments.

Instead, students participate in a year-long engineering engagement.

Working individually or within small interdisciplinary teams, students design,
implement, evaluate, and continuously refine an enterprise cognitive
architecture addressing a realistic organizational problem.

Projects evolve throughout the semester as new constraints, regulations,
business objectives, and technical challenges are introduced.

Students are expected to revise both implementation and architecture rather
than merely extend software.

---

# Fireside Conversations

Each week concludes with a moderated graduate seminar.

Example questions include:

> Can an organization possess memory independent of its employees?

> Should enterprise AI be permitted to modify its own ontology?

> If every employee leaves, what remains of organizational knowledge?

> Can trust exist without provenance?

> Is an enterprise cognitive architecture fundamentally different from a
human cognitive architecture?

> Should intelligent organizations optimize for efficiency or resilience?

> What responsibilities can never be delegated to autonomous systems?

Students are expected to defend positions using ideas drawn from the entire
curriculum.

# Modern Perspective

The future of Artificial Intelligence will not be determined solely by larger
models or faster hardware.

It will be determined by our ability to engineer organizations in which
humans and intelligent systems cooperate responsibly over decades.

Applied Cognitive Engineering therefore represents the study of intelligent
enterprises rather than intelligent software.
